# First-tranche causal formation rules (research proposal)

## What can the sources justify, and what remains a project choice?

### Takeaway

The literature gives reproducible *examples* of extrema-based definitions, and practitioner sources agree on broad geometry and close-based breakout, but no source establishes one universal or Vietnam-validated parameter set. The numeric cards below are proposed **registration choices**, not empirically optimized values or claims of predictive power.

### Cited Findings

- Lo, Mamaysky and Wang formalized triangles, rectangles and double tops/bottoms using sequences of local extrema. Their US study used a 35-session formation window plus a three-session detection lag; it explicitly separated pattern completion from when it could be detected. Their double-level rule used peaks/troughs within 1.5% and at least 22 trading sessions between tests; their rectangle rule used extrema within 0.75%. These are features of *their* US algorithm, not Vietnamese calibration. — [Lo, Mamaysky and Wang, pp. 11–14](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/19268/Lo-Mamaysky_wang_foundations.pdf)
- Their conditional-return result is not equivalent to an implementable excess-profit result; the paper explicitly distinguishes informativeness from profitability. — [Lo, Mamaysky and Wang, p. 17 and conclusion](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/19268/Lo-Mamaysky_wang_foundations.pdf)
- In practitioner terminology a double top/bottom is only *completed* after the intervening support/resistance breaks. A triple top uses the lowest intervening trough as support; a triple bottom uses the highest intervening peak as resistance. — [StockCharts double top](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/chart-analysis/chart-patterns/double-top-reversal), [double bottom](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/chart-analysis/chart-patterns/double-bottom-reversal), [triple top](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/chart-analysis/chart-patterns/triple-top-reversal), [triple bottom](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/chart-analysis/chart-patterns/triple-bottom-reversal)
- Symmetrical triangles have falling highs/rising lows; ascending ones have roughly level highs/rising lows; descending ones have falling highs/roughly level lows. Rectangles have comparable highs and lows. The *breakout side*, rather than the drawing's bullish/bearish nickname, is the observed resolution. — [CME education](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/courses/technical-analysis/trend-and-continuation-patterns), [StockCharts triangle](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/chart-analysis/chart-patterns/symmetrical-triangle), [rectangle](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/chart-analysis/chart-patterns/rectangle)
- A flag is a short parallel-sided pause following a sharp pole; a pennant is a short converging-sided pause after one. Practitioner guides differ on the upper duration (StockCharts describes one to twelve weeks and an ideal of one to four; Bulkowski calls three weeks an arbitrary maximum). Thus neither duration is a scientific constant. — [StockCharts flag/pennant](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/chart-analysis/chart-patterns/flag-pennant), [Bulkowski book chapter](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/9781119203537.ch11)
- Practitioners sometimes add breakout volume or multi-day/percentage price filters, but these are alternative confirmation rules, not evidence that a particular filter works in Vietnam. — [StockCharts rectangle](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/chart-analysis/chart-patterns/rectangle), [double top](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/chart-analysis/chart-patterns/double-top-reversal)

### Inferences

- Register one price-only close-confirmed specification first. Treat any breakout-volume condition, wider tolerance, or extra duration as an additional, counted hypothesis. This preserves an interpretable first result while honoring the project's matched-volume requirement when volume is eventually tested.
- Do not import the Lo study's 35-session window wholesale: it would exclude many multiweek patterns the user explicitly wants to understand. Its published 1.5%, 0.75%, and 22-session values are useful *starting references*, not evidence that those choices maximize Vietnamese performance.

### Gaps

- No primary study found that independently validates these exact multiweek-rule thresholds on adjusted HOSE/HNX/UPCoM equities, with matched volume, trading restrictions and costs. Historical pattern incidence and economic value are therefore unknown until the registered census and forward record.
- Sources do not settle whether closes or intraday highs/lows are the best anchors. The close-only choice below is a transparent implementation choice; high/low geometry would be another trial, not a silent substitute.

## What fixed, inspectable rule cards should be registered first?

### Takeaway

Use one shared causal close-pivot primitive and four cards: repeated levels, full-sized triangles, rectangles, and short pole-plus-pause overlays (flag/pennant). This covers the requested variants without pretending each drawing is an independent event.

### Cited Findings

- Five alternating extrema are sufficient to define a version of triangle or rectangle geometry in the Lo algorithm; its particular smoothing and lag are methodological choices. — [Lo, Mamaysky and Wang, §II](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/19268/Lo-Mamaysky_wang_foundations.pdf)
- Practitioner accounts require at least two touches per triangle side and comparable repeated upper/lower levels for rectangles; they distinguish flags from pennants primarily by parallel versus converging boundaries after a sharp move. — [StockCharts triangle](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/chart-analysis/chart-patterns/symmetrical-triangle), [rectangle](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/chart-analysis/chart-patterns/rectangle), [flag/pennant](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/chart-analysis/chart-patterns/flag-pennant)

### Inferences

**Common card v0 (all numbers in this subsection are proposed design choices, not sourced Vietnamese facts).** For each eligible symbol and uninterrupted adjusted-price segment, index sessions by `t`; use adjusted daily **close** `C[t]` for anchors and for break confirmation. A high pivot at `i` is the maximum of `C[i-2..i+2]`; a low pivot is the minimum. Ties elect the **earliest** date in that five-session window. The pivot is usable only at close `i+2` (its `confirmed_on`). Require ≥3 sessions between successive anchors. In a run of same-type pivots, retain the more extreme one for the *still-forming* episode (earliest on equality); never revise a previously emitted signal. Use five **alternating, consecutive confirmed** pivot types for the bounded patterns. Source-quality failures, unexplained adjustments, and genuine missing trading sessions terminate a segment rather than being spanned. The first possible signal is the close of a day on or after the last anchor's `confirmed_on`; a crossing observed before then is recorded as `late_or_unobservable`, not backdated. Zero look-ahead requires recomputing through day `t` to reproduce exactly the day-`t` state.

**Shared state/events.** `forming` begins only at the last anchor's `confirmed_on`, with formation ID `(symbol, first-anchor date, first-anchor type)` and versioned anchors. The *first* close at least 0.5% beyond an already knowable upper or lower boundary produces `confirmed_up` or `confirmed_down` at that close; the next eligible open is the earliest modeled entry. On each close, compare with the prior close and prior known boundary snapshot to distinguish a new crossing from a structure that was already outside before detection. If both boundary tests appear true (bad geometry or adjusted data), reject and audit rather than choosing a side. Do not require relative volume for the price-only primary event. A contrary crossing before the named expected resolution is a contradictory outcome, **not** a dropped candidate.

**Repeated level — double/triple top and bottom.** For a top use `H1–L1–H2`; for a bottom mirror `L1–H1–L2`. First and last like extrema must be 22–120 sessions apart and within 1.5% of their average. The intervening swing must have depth ≥5% relative to that average. `candidate_on = confirmed_on(H2/L2)`. Double-top neckline is intervening `L1`, double-bottom neckline is intervening `H1`. Confirm when `C[t] < neckline × 0.995` for tops or `C[t] > neckline × 1.005` for bottoms, within 30 sessions of the candidate. Before that, close > highest top ×1.005 (or < lowest bottom ×0.995) is a separately retained *opposite-side* resolution. For a triple, require `H1–L1–H2–L2–H3` (mirror for bottom), each adjacent pair of like extrema 22–90 sessions apart, whole span ≤180, all three like extrema within 1.5% of their average, and both intervening swings ≥5% deep. Confirm below `min(L1,L2) × 0.995` for a triple top or above `max(H1,H2) × 1.005` for a triple bottom. The pair-of-valleys equality is **not** required; the conservative min/max neckline prevents declaring a break while an earlier support/resistance still holds. If a third test forms before any double-level resolution, upgrade the still-forming episode to a triple alias with its original ID, not a second independent bet. A reversal *context* label requires the 60-session pre-formation close return ≥+15% for a top or ≤−15% for a bottom; lacking that, retain the geometry but label its prior trend `other/unknown`.

**Triangle — symmetric/ascending/descending.** Require five alternating pivots across 15–90 sessions (first-to-last anchor). Connect first and last high pivots into upper line `U(t)`, first and last low pivots into lower line `L(t)`, by linear interpolation/extrapolation in **log adjusted close versus trading-session index**. The middle pivot on a three-touch side must lie within 1% of its line at its date. Let `W(t) = 2×[U(t)−L(t)]/[U(t)+L(t)]` in price space, and `ΔU = U(last)/U(first)−1` and analogously `ΔL` (evaluate both lines at the first and last anchor dates). Require `W(first) ≥5%`, `1.5% ≤W(last) ≤0.75×W(first)`, with `U>L` throughout the observed episode. Flat means absolute boundary drift ≤1%; rising means drift ≥1%; falling means drift ≤−1%. Classify **symmetric** = falling upper+rising lower, **ascending** = flat upper+rising lower, **descending** = falling upper+flat lower. (Exact 1% boundary ties are assigned to flat, so use strict `>1%`/`<−1%` for rising/falling in implementation.) Freeze the two lines at `candidate_on` (last pivot confirmation). Confirm the first close outside `U(t)×1.005` or `L(t)×0.995`; record actual side even if opposite the conventional bias. Candidate expires at the earliest of 20 sessions after candidate, total 90 sessions from first anchor, or first projected date where `U≤L` (apex). No signal at or after the apex.

**Rectangle.** Require five alternating pivots over 20–120 sessions; all high pivots within 0.75% of their mean and all low pivots within 0.75% of their mean; `(min highs − max lows)/midpoint ≥5%`. Freeze `U=max(high pivots)` and `L=min(low pivots)` at candidate confirmation. Confirm first close >`U×1.005` or <`L×0.995` within 30 sessions after candidate and before the 120-session total-age cap. There is no presumed breakout direction. A close sitting in the range after candidate remains `forming` even when the rectangle had a prior uptrend.

**Flag/pennant: contextual overlays, not separate underlying episodes.** The pole is exactly the **10-session** change ending one session before the first pause anchor: bull `C[s−1]/C[s−11] ≥1.10`, bear ≤`1/1.10`; `s` is the first pause anchor date. Require five alternating pause pivots spanning 12–25 sessions, with no more than 50% arithmetic retracement of pole height by any pause close and initial pause width ≥2%. A **flag** has two boundary lines fitted from first/last highs and first/last lows as above, both move at least 1% *against* the pole, ending width is 0.8–1.2×starting width, and pause width ≤50% of pole's absolute price height. A **pennant** has symmetric-triangle geometry, but with `W(first) ≥5%`, `W(last) ≥1.5%` and ≤0.75×first width, and the pole/retracement/12–25-session requirements. Freeze bounds at candidate date; register an **in-pole-direction** breakout as a continuation result, and an opposite-side breakout as contradiction; keep both. Expire after 10 sessions from candidate or at projected apex for a pennant. The pole is a price move only; matched relative volume at pole/pause/break is stored as a separate descriptive vector, not an untested filter.

**Validation math to include in rule card.** Durations are counted in valid exchange trading sessions, not calendar days. Price-level comparisons use unrounded adjusted series for research, with adjustment-factor provenance and exchange tick/limit metadata kept for tradability analysis; do not round adjusted historical prices to a current tick grid. Store every anchor's occurrence date and `confirmed_on`, plus boundary snapshot, candidate date, signal side/date, expiry reason, data exclusions, and detector version.

### Gaps

- These are **unapproved candidate thresholds**. They need Ben's architectural sign-off and a hand-worked fixture audit before a historical census. The 0.5% breakout margin, 5% depth/width, 10% pole, 60-session context, and expiry periods have not been validated for Vietnamese tick ladders, price limits, volatility regimes, or sparse historical names.
- The pivot operator on raw closes is intentionally simpler than Lo's smoothed extrema. It may miss wick-based or visually obvious patterns and can make five-pivot flags rare. Report detection coverage and misses before altering it; do not tune on the spent holdout.

## How should event dates, ambiguity and overlap be handled?

### Takeaway

An attractive historical drawing is not evidence that it was knowable then. The event table needs append-only dated states, an explicit failure taxonomy, and one episode-level unit even when several labels apply.

### Cited Findings

- Lo, Mamaysky and Wang built a lag into detection because the final extremum is not observable as a completed pattern on its own date; they start conditional returns only after the lag. — [Lo, Mamaysky and Wang, §II.B](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/19268/Lo-Mamaysky_wang_foundations.pdf)
- A practitioner description warns that a triple bottom can initially resemble a double bottom, a descending triangle or a rectangle, and treats it as neutral until the resistance break. — [StockCharts triple bottom](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/chart-analysis/chart-patterns/triple-bottom-reversal)
- The standard triangle and rectangle descriptions say that the true breakout direction is unknown until the exit; flags and pennants are conventionally continuation hypotheses, but that does not exclude opposite breaks. — [StockCharts triangle](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/chart-analysis/chart-patterns/symmetrical-triangle), [rectangle](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/chart-analysis/chart-patterns/rectangle), [flag/pennant](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/chart-analysis/chart-patterns/flag-pennant)

### Inferences

- Retain `candidate_on`, `confirmation_on`, `expiry_on`, and `failure_on` as different fields. `expiry` = no clean crossing before deadline; `pre-confirmation invalidation` = opposite-side resolution or loss of valid geometry before confirmation; `post-confirmation failure` = close at least 0.5% **back inside the frozen breakout boundary** within 10 sessions after confirmation. These latter events are labelled on the date observed and never erase the original signal or its subsequent return. A retest that touches but does not close back inside is not automatically failure.
- Same raw episode can expose several labels: a pennant is a pole-conditioned symmetric triangle; a double top can become a triple top; a triple test can sit in a rectangle. Save all labels as a fingerprint but select a deterministic canonical family for independent counts: `flag/pennant` over its underlying geometry; `triple` over `double` while still forming; and `rectangle` over repeated-level if there are two comparable highs **and** lows. Keep all raw labels in descriptive outputs. Do not count those labels as independent samples or multiply a stock-day's statistical weight.
- Use the same breakout event ID when several family labels share the same anchors and boundary crossing. Different nearby windows on one stock are de-clustered by overlapping anchor span plus shared break; allow a new event only after the prior episode resolves or expires and a fresh non-overlapping first anchor appears. Simultaneous genuinely distinct events on one day remain in the stock-day fingerprint but the one-pick-per-day policy is tested separately.
- Repeated runs truncated at each candidate/signal date must yield identical past events. Audit examples where the 2-right-bar pivot confirmation coincides with a crossing; where the price crossed before the last pivot was confirmed; where five pivots are barely too few/too many; where adjusted data revise an old bar; and where floor/ceiling or no-match sessions make entry/exit untradeable. A traditional bullish name resolving down remains in the denominator.

### Gaps

- Whether opposite-side resolution should be called `invalidated` or `confirmed_opposite` in the production API is a semantic decision for Ben; the data must retain the side and date either way.
- Exact price-limit, historical tick-size, and venue-era execution checks are beyond this formation-only card and must be supplied by the separate outcome/tradability protocol. These checks cannot be inferred from pattern geometry alone.
