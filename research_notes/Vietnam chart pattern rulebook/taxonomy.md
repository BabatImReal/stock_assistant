# Chart pattern taxonomy and causal definitions

## Which families are truly distinct versus variants or aliases?

### Takeaway
The useful unit is a small set of observable geometries, not every named drawing. Direction, preceding trend, scale, and breakout side are attributes of a formation; counting each drawing as an independent discovery would duplicate events.

### Cited Findings
- Lo, Mamaysky and Wang formalized five *pairs* from sequences of local extrema: head and shoulders, broadening formations, triangles, rectangles, and double tops/bottoms. Their US study found some conditional return distributions differed from unconditional ones, without establishing a universal profitable pattern. — [Authors' abstract](https://www.mit.edu/~alo/Papers/techanal.html); [Journal of Finance paper](https://bashtage.github.io/kevinsheppard.com/files/teaching/mfe/advanced-econometrics/Lo_Mamaysky_Wang.pdf)
- IG lists head and shoulders, double tops/bottoms, rounding bottom, cup and handle, wedges, flags/pennants, and three triangle variants. It says a decisive close beyond the relevant level and ideally greater volume provide conventional confirmation; it expressly says these patterns are not guarantees. — [IG chart pattern guide](https://www.ig.com/uk/trading-strategies/10-chart-patterns-every-trader-needs-to-know-190514)
- CMC distinguishes a double top's second failed high from completion below the intervening low; a double bottom completes above the intervening high. It treats a pennant as a small triangle after a sharp move, and a flag as a small channel after a sharp move. — [CMC chart pattern guide](https://www.cmcmarkets.com/en/shares/stock-chart-patterns)
- Investing.com describes ascending/descending/symmetrical triangles by flat or sloping edges, flags as tilted rectangles after a pole, pennants as small triangles after a pole, and triple tops/bottoms as three tests of a level. — [Investing.com pattern guide](https://www.investing.com/academy/analysis/top-10-stock-chart-patterns/)
- The project already has `tight_range`, `inside_day_run`, `higher_lows` against flat highs, and a price-only close above the previous 20 highs. They are short structures, not complete multiweek chart formations. — [Existing source: consolidation.py](../../src/vnstock_research/patterns/consolidation.py); [Project knowledge §3.4](../../docs/knowledge/pattern-research-knowledge.md)

### Inferences
- Suggested canonical families and variant fields, all **research definitions to register before testing**:

  | Family | Observable skeleton | Named variants and overlap | Prior trend? | Forming versus confirmed |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Repeated horizontal test | Two or three distinct confirmed highs near a common resistance, or lows near a common support, separated by a meaningful opposite swing | Double and triple top/bottom; a rectangle with exactly two or three touches can overlap | Required to call a *reversal*; otherwise repeated-level structure | Forming after the final level test; confirmed only by close beyond the intervening neckline/support or resistance |
  | Three-extrema reversal | Shoulder, higher head, shoulder with two intervening lows; mirror for inverse | Head and shoulders/inverse; an almost equal three-peak structure may also be triple top | Required for reversal interpretation | Forming once the right shoulder is confirmed; confirmed on close through the time-aligned neckline |
  | Converging bounds | At least two causal highs and two causal lows define a narrowing band | Symmetrical, ascending, descending triangles; rising/falling wedges; a *small triangle after a pole* is a pennant | Not essential to classify geometry; required for a continuation label | Forming while closes remain within bounds; confirmed by close outside a previously fixed/projected boundary |
  | Parallel or flat bounds | Two touch-supported lines with roughly stable separation | Horizontal rectangle, up/down channel; *short countertrend channel after a pole* is a flag; very tight flat rectangle may be called a base | Required for a flag; optional for rectangle/channel | Confirmed by close outside the boundary, with direction recorded |
  | Broadening bounds | Successive swing highs and lows move farther apart | Broadening top/bottom, expanding triangle, megaphone | Trend context is descriptive; name alone does not fix exit side | Confirmed only by an observed boundary break; may be too noisy/rare for a primary test |
  | Rounded turn/base | Gradual decline, flatter trough, gradual rise toward old rim; mirror for rounded top | Saucer/rounding bottom; *cup and handle* adds a later, smaller pullback below the rim; inverted cup/handle mirrors it | Cup continuation label requires earlier rise; rounded bottom can also follow decline | A cup candidate requires both sides of the bowl; cup-and-handle confirmed only after the handle and close above rim/handle resistance |
  | Boundary event | Close through a previously known level or trendline | Breakout, breakdown, false breakout, failed breakdown, retest | Not needed for event itself | First crossing is dated immediately; failure is a **later** event dated when price returns inside or crosses opposite boundary |

- Use a hierarchy, not mutually exclusive flags. A five-day pennant is also a small triangle; a bull flag is a small channel; a cup embeds a rounded base; an ascending triangle can share the project's `higher_lows` flag; an inverse head and shoulders can contain a double bottom. Keep both raw flags, but treat the same symbol/date/formation span as one clustered event when measuring independent evidence. Preserve the underlying family and all aliases as descriptive labels.
- “Triple top” is a repeated-level variant, not inherently stronger than a double top. Its third test adds time and may change sample selection; the claimed reliability in the practitioner guide is a hypothesis for Vietnam, not a fact.
- The user images enumerate symmetrical, ascending and descending triangles; rising/falling wedges; flags/pennants; rectangles; head and shoulders/inverse; double/triple tops/bottoms; cup/inverse cup and handle; channels. Their schematics are naming references, not observations of prices or probabilities.

### Gaps
- No reliable source provides one canonical threshold or exhaustive taxonomy accepted across researchers. The literature itself identifies ambiguity of pattern specifications as a methodological problem. — [Formal chart-pattern classification paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S002002551631636X)
- Existing sources do not establish that any of these long formations beat Vietnamese stock-specific base rates. Do not attach probabilities to names.

## What observable geometry and confirmation event characterize each?

### Takeaway
Price geometry should decide candidate status; a subsequent close beyond a causally known level decides confirmation. Matched volume is a separately measured attribute of formation and breakout, because making it mandatory would prevent comparison of price-only and volume-confirmed versions.

### Cited Findings
- CMC defines the neckline event explicitly for double top/bottom and head and shoulders, and describes converging highs/lows for triangles. — [CMC chart pattern guide](https://www.cmcmarkets.com/en/shares/stock-chart-patterns)
- IG describes rising wedges as two rising lines with steeper support, falling wedges as two falling lines with steeper resistance, and triangles as flat plus sloping or two converging edges. It notes a symmetrical triangle may resolve either way. — [IG chart pattern guide](https://www.ig.com/uk/trading-strategies/10-chart-patterns-every-trader-needs-to-know-190514)
- The project's knowledge document requires adjusted prices, matched rather than negotiated volume, and measuring volume-confirmed patterns beside price-only patterns. — [Project knowledge §4.2–4.3, §7.5](../../docs/knowledge/pattern-research-knowledge.md)
- Osler's head-and-shoulders study found unusual US trading volume near neckline crossing, while trading based on the pattern was unprofitable in its sample; its pattern-identification sensitivity analyses changed asymmetry and volume rules. — [Federal Reserve Bank of New York study](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr42.pdf)

### Inferences
- Geometry proposal for registration: Detect alternating, confirmed high/low pivots on adjusted OHLC (choose explicitly whether extremes or closes anchor geometry). For two similar peaks use a documented price tolerance, minimum peak separation and minimum valley depth; for a head and shoulders require the middle high to exceed both shoulders and an admissible shoulder-height difference; mirror low geometry for bullish versions. Determine neckline from the intervening pivots, and at each date evaluate that line using only anchors confirmed by then. Reject degenerate flat/noise moves by a separately chosen minimum movement relative to dated tick or pre-formation volatility.
- For triangles and wedges, require at least two confirmed touches on each boundary, correct slope signs, narrowing distance over the formation, and no pre-confirmation close materially outside either boundary. Ascending triangle: approximately flat upper edge and rising lower edge. Descending: flat lower, falling upper. Symmetrical: falling upper, rising lower. Rising wedge: both rise but lower line rises faster; falling wedge: both fall but upper line falls faster. A triangle can break in either direction; record actual break side even when opposed to the traditional name. A wedge's slope does not determine the outcome in the data.
- Rectangle and channel: repeated visits to upper and lower boundaries with approximately constant band width; rectangle has approximately flat bounds, channel slopes together. Flag: an objectively defined prior impulse (“pole”) plus a short, bounded pause whose slope opposes or is flatter than the pole. Pennant: same pole followed by a short converging pause. Absence of a pole makes them ordinary channel/triangle candidates. Do not require the “flagpole target” as a measured outcome; target-before-stop must be separately registered.
- Rounded base: require a gradual left descent and right ascent with a broad enough low region, rather than declaring any two lows a cup. Cup adds approximately aligned rims; handle adds a shorter, shallower pullback after the right rim. Inverse versions mirror all price inequalities. These are the most arbitrary rules and should start as an explicitly lower-priority family until the geometry is inspectable and sample counts are known.
- Event states should be `none`, `forming`, `confirmed_up`, `confirmed_down`, `invalidated`, and `expired` (or equivalent dated events). “Forming” is not a tradable confirmation. A breakout or breakdown is a price close beyond the relevant fixed level at day *t*. Store signal date *t*, formation start/end, confirmation date for every pivot, anchors, boundary value at *t*, and price/volume features on *t* so an analyst can reproduce the flag. A wick alone remains a separate `intraday_probe` candidate if studied.
- Volume variables: matched-volume ratio during formation versus a **pre-formation** baseline, volume trend/dry-up during the pause, volume ratio on the boundary crossing against the **previous** sessions, plus up/down matched-volume balance. Do not mix negotiated blocks. Price-only and price-plus-volume are separate hypotheses; formation volume can be missing while price geometry remains observable, but no missing value should silently count as low volume.
- Candidate scale bands to investigate, not accepted truth: 3–15 trading sessions for flag/pennant, about 10–60 for double/triple tests and triangles, and roughly 30–200 for rounded/cup formations. The existing project is aimed at 3–5 day outcomes, so multiweek formations require separately registered 10/20/40-session observation windows if those are tested. Formation duration, pivot radius, tolerance, slope, depth, allowed breakout lag, volume ratio, and outcome horizons are **parameters needing preregistration**, not optimized facts.

### Gaps
- Practitioner sources disagree on wedge and pennant naming and on whether the same geometry is continuation or reversal. This is why a measured prior trend plus observed breakout side is safer than a hard-coded direction. — [IG guide](https://www.ig.com/uk/trading-strategies/10-chart-patterns-every-trader-needs-to-know-190514); [CMC guide](https://www.cmcmarkets.com/en/shares/stock-chart-patterns)
- The guides give few reproducible bounds for “round,” “sharp,” “shallow,” or “decisive.” Those terms require precommitted numerical choices and sensitivity checks on discovery data only.

## How can completed patterns be defined point-in-time with a known signal date?

### Takeaway
The pivotal distinction is the date a turning point occurred versus the later date it became knowable. A detector may label a historical pivot, but it must not use that pivot in a signal until its confirmation date; the price crossing that follows is the actual pattern signal date.

### Cited Findings
- The current project implementation already recognizes this timing issue: a pivot at row *j* needs *k* later rows and the support/resistance calculation excludes pivots whose confirmation window has not closed. — [Existing source: trend.py](../../src/vnstock_research/features/trend.py)
- Lo, Mamaysky and Wang's original algorithm was a systematic computer recognition method based on local extrema; this establishes a research precedent for geometry as explicit extrema relations, but its results are from US shares in 1962–1996 and are not Vietnam results. — [Authors' abstract](https://www.mit.edu/~alo/Papers/techanal.html); [Journal of Finance paper](https://bashtage.github.io/kevinsheppard.com/files/teaching/mfe/advanced-econometrics/Lo_Mamaysky_Wang.pdf)
- IG's guide distinguishes an intraday probe from a close beyond a level and recommends confirmation; CMC dates double top/bottom completion on the crossing, not merely the second high/low. — [IG guide](https://www.ig.com/uk/trading-strategies/10-chart-patterns-every-trader-needs-to-know-190514); [CMC guide](https://www.cmcmarkets.com/en/shares/stock-chart-patterns)

### Inferences
- A causal pivot implementation can reuse the existing `features/trend.py` concept: pivot extreme at *j* is timestamped *j* but becomes eligible at *j+k* after the right-side bars close. For a live signal at *t*, every pivot anchor must have `confirmed_on <= t`; any line using a later anchor is inadmissible. A left-only trailing extreme may be a *candidate* in real time, but should not be retrospectively relabelled as a confirmed pivot at its original date.
- Freeze the candidate's geometry at the latest known anchor set before testing today's crossing. On date *t*, calculate the neckline or boundary projection from that frozen set, then compare adjusted close *t* to it (with prechosen tolerance, if any). Do not fit the line using the breakout bar or a later pivot. If a right shoulder/second bottom confirms on *t* and price also crosses on *t*, specify event order and use only information through the close of *t*; earliest modeled entry is a later tradable session.
- Handle invalidation separately. Examples: a double-top candidate posts a distinctly higher third peak before breaking its neckline; a triangle closes through one side and then returns inside; a cup handle falls below the chosen allowable depth. A “false breakout” exists only when the later reversal has happened, and must be dated then, not backdated to the initial crossing. A failed bullish breakout is adverse information for a long-only investor; it is not automatically a short-trade recommendation.
- Permit overlapping candidates but prevent one formation from producing a fresh signal on every subsequent day: assign stable formation identity from symbol, initial pivots and scale; allow one first-crossing event per boundary/direction until invalidated or expired. Record both raw candidate count and deduplicated formation/event count. A double top that evolves into a triple top can be represented as a later variant of the same level-test episode, rather than two independent “wins.”
- Quarantine any candidate whose adjusted-price chain, matched volume when used, exchange/session calendar, or source reconciliation is defective. Do not bridge a suspension or missing trading bar inside the formation or outcome interval. Corporate-action re-adjustment requires versioned inputs and recomputation/reconciliation before historical results are reused. These conditions follow the project's existing data and validation contracts, not pattern folklore.
- The traditional “bullish”/“bearish” label is a proposed mechanism only. A head and shoulders needs an identifiable earlier rise to be called a bearish *reversal*; without it the detector can record its geometry but not its reversal context. An ascending triangle below a falling MA200 or a falling wedge breaking down remains an observed structure with conflicting context. Do not drop the contradictory cases from research; stratify them.

### Gaps
- No published source establishes that one pivot radius, breakout tolerance, formation time limit, or volume cutoff is optimal for HOSE, HNX and UPCoM. Those are study choices, and repeated testing of them will consume discovery data.
- The existing project has only short-consolidation flags and a 20-day breakout, so a complete formation lifecycle and causal event ledger are proposed work, not current capability. — [Existing source: consolidation.py](../../src/vnstock_research/patterns/consolidation.py)
